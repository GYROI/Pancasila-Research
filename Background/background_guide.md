# Background Guide: Analisis Risiko Kesehatan Fisik pada Pengguna Transportasi Publik yang Padat dari Perspektif SDG3

## 1. Introduction
This research aims to analyze the physical health risks faced by users of crowded public transportation from the perspective of Sustainable Development Goal 3 (SDG3): "Ensure healthy lives and promote well-being for all at all ages." Public transportation crowding is a prevalent issue in many urban areas, particularly in developing countries, and poses significant health challenges that need to be understood and mitigated to achieve SDG3 targets.

## 2. Background on SDG3
Sustainable Development Goal 3 focuses on ensuring healthy lives and promoting well-being for all ages. Key targets include:
- Reducing maternal mortality
- Ending preventable deaths of newborns and children under 5
- Combating communicable diseases
- Reducing mortality from non-communicable diseases
- Promoting mental health and well-being
- Halving global deaths and injuries from road traffic accidents
- Achieving universal health coverage
- Reducing illnesses and deaths from hazardous chemicals and pollution

In the context of public transportation, SDG3 is relevant because crowded transit environments can exacerbate health risks, undermine well-being, and hinder access to essential health services.

## 3. Context of Crowded Public Transportation
Public transportation crowding occurs when demand exceeds supply, leading to conditions where passengers experience:
- High density (people per square meter)
- Prolonged standing time
- Limited ventilation
- Increased physical contact
- Stress and discomfort

Crowding is especially common in:
- Developing countries with rapid urbanization and inadequate infrastructure
- Peak hours in major cities
- Informal or paratransit systems (e.g., minibuses, shared taxis)

## 4. Physical Health Risks Associated with Crowded Public Transport
Several physical health risks have been documented in relation to crowded public transportation:

### 4.1 Infectious Disease Transmission
- Crowded, poorly ventilated spaces facilitate the spread of airborne infections (e.g., tuberculosis, influenza, COVID-19).
- High-touch surfaces increase the risk of fomite transmission.
- Studies during the COVID-19 pandemic highlighted public transport as a potential venue for superspreading events.

### 4.2 Musculoskeletal Disorders
- Prolonged standing and awkward postures in crowded vehicles can lead to back pain, neck strain, and other musculoskeletal issues.
- Sudden movements (e.g., braking, acceleration) increase the risk of falls and injuries.

### 4.3 Cardiovascular Stress
- Crowding-induced stress and anxiety can elevate heart rate and blood pressure.
- Exposure to air pollution (particulate matter, nitrogen oxides) in congested traffic contributes to cardiovascular strain.

### 4.4 Exposure to Air Pollution
- In-cabin concentrations of pollutants can be high, especially in older vehicles or those with poor ventilation.
- Passengers are exposed to both ambient traffic pollution and emissions from the vehicle itself.

### 4.5 Heat Stress and Dehydration
- In hot climates, crowded vehicles can lead to overheating, dehydration, and heat-related illnesses, particularly for vulnerable populations (elderly, children, those with pre-existing conditions).

### 4.6 Noise-Induced Stress
- High noise levels from crowding, vehicle engines, and external traffic can cause stress, hearing damage, and sleep disturbances.

## 5. Relevance to SDG3
Analyzing these risks through the lens of SDG3 is crucial because:
- It links transportation policy directly to health outcomes.
- It highlights the need for interventions that promote healthy lives (SDG3.8: universal health coverage) and reduce illness from pollution (SDG3.9).
- It supports the development of sustainable, healthy cities (SDG11) by improving the safety and healthiness of public transport.
- Addressing crowding can improve access to health services and education, contributing to multiple SDGs.

## 6. Research Gaps and Objectives
While there is literature on individual risks (e.g., air pollution, infectious diseases), there is a need for comprehensive studies that:
- Assess multiple physical health risks simultaneously in crowded transit environments.
- Focus on vulnerable populations (low-income commuters, women, elderly).
- Evaluate the effectiveness of interventions (e.g., improved ventilation, reduced crowding, better vehicle design) from a health perspective.
- Integrate findings into policy recommendations aligned with SDG3 targets.

## 7. Proposed Methodology (Outline)
A mixed-methods approach could include:
- **Quantitative**: Surveys, health screenings, environmental monitoring (air quality, noise levels) on crowded routes.
- **Qualitative**: Focus groups and interviews with commuters to capture perceived health impacts and coping strategies.
- **Data Analysis**: Statistical modeling to correlate crowding levels with health indicators.
- **Policy Review**: Analysis of existing transportation and health policies for alignment with SDG3.

## 8. Expected Outcomes
- A detailed assessment of physical health risks among crowded public transport users.
- Evidence-based recommendations for transport operators, urban planners, and public health officials.
- Contribution to the literature on transport-related health risks and SDG3 implementation.
- Potential to inform guidelines for healthy and sustainable public transport systems.
